# Working with Support Tickets

If you need help from the Flowdesk support team, open a support ticket. Tickets keep all your messages and updates about a problem in one place.

## Chat assistant or support ticket?

The chat assistant is the fastest way to get answers about your own account, such as your plan, renewal date, invoices, payments, and the status of your tickets.

It can't change your account or issue refunds. Open a ticket when you need:

- A refund, or a review of a charge
- An account change you can't make in Settings
- Help with a bug or an integration that this help center doesn't resolve
- A person to look at something unusual

## Opening a ticket

1. Select **Help** in the sidebar, then **My tickets**.
2. Select **New ticket**.
3. Choose a category.
4. Write a short subject and describe the problem.
5. Add any screenshots or files.
6. Select **Submit**.

You'll get an email confirming your ticket number, such as `ticket_123`. If you can't sign in, email support@flowdesk.example from the address on your account, and we'll open a ticket for you.

### Choosing a category

| Category  | Use it for                                                  |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Billing   | Charges, invoices, payments, refunds, and plan pricing      |
| Technical | Bugs, errors, integrations, and unexpected product behavior |
| Account   | Sign-in, your profile, team members, and canceling          |
| General   | Anything else, including how-to and product questions       |

### Writing a helpful ticket

The more we know, the less back-and-forth you'll need. A good ticket includes:

- What you were trying to do
- What happened, including the exact wording of any error message
- When it started, and whether it happens every time
- Who's affected: only you, or your whole team
- The steps we can follow to see it ourselves

For billing problems, include the invoice and payment numbers. For integration problems, include the name of the integration and of any automation rule involved.

**Please don't include sensitive details.** Never send a full card number, password, or recovery code. We'll never ask for them.

## How priority is set

Our team sets each ticket's priority based on how it affects you.

| Priority | When it applies                                          | Examples                                                                                                        |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| High     | You're blocked from working, or a charge looks incorrect | A duplicate charge, automation rules that have stopped running, an overdue notice that could affect your access |
| Medium   | You have a problem but can keep working                  | A declined card, an integration that keeps asking you to reconnect                                              |
| Low      | A general question or minor request                      | Finding invoices, adding a VAT number, changing an email                                                        |

Priority can change as we learn more. If a problem gets worse, reply to the ticket and let us know.

## Response times

Support is available Monday to Friday, 09:00 to 18:00 UTC. All plans include email support, and the Business plan also includes priority support, which puts your tickets ahead in the queue.

These are our targets for a first response, not for a full resolution.

| Priority | Starter and Pro | Business         |
| -------- | --------------- | ---------------- |
| High     | 1 business day  | 4 business hours |
| Medium   | 2 business days | 1 business day   |
| Low      | 3 business days | 2 business days  |

Complex problems can take longer to resolve, particularly if they involve another company's service. We'll keep you updated on the ticket.

## Ticket statuses

| Status      | What it means                                                                |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Open        | We've received your ticket and it's waiting for a specialist to pick it up   |
| In progress | A specialist is working on it, or waiting on a confirmation or a third party |
| Resolved    | We've answered the question or fixed the problem                             |

An **Open** ticket can already have an initial reply, which might ask for more information. Replying to it helps us pick it up sooner.

## How a ticket moves through our team

When you submit a ticket, it goes into a shared queue with the status **Open**. Our team reviews new tickets in order of priority, and then by age, so a high-priority ticket opened today is looked at before a low-priority one opened yesterday. Business customers' tickets go ahead of others with the same priority. If your ticket clearly belongs in a different category, or the priority doesn't match the problem you've described, we'll change it during this first review. You don't need to do anything when that happens, and it doesn't send your ticket to the back of the queue.

A specialist then picks up the ticket and the status changes to **In progress**. Billing tickets go to the billing team, who can see your invoices and payment attempts, so they can often answer without needing anything further from you. Technical tickets can take longer, because the specialist usually needs to reproduce the problem first. That's why the steps you followed, the error wording, and the time it happened are so useful. If the problem turns out to involve another company's service, such as a calendar provider, the specialist may need to wait for them. Some account changes also have to wait for a confirmation, such as a new billing email address being verified. Your ticket stays **In progress** during these waits, even if you don't hear from us every day.

When the question is answered or the problem is fixed, the specialist explains what happened and marks the ticket **Resolved**. Sometimes the answer depends on a decision that only the support team can make, such as a refund review, and in that case the reply tells you the outcome and any next steps. Resolving a ticket doesn't close the conversation for good, because you have 14 days to reply if it didn't fix things. Our team also reads your replies on Resolved tickets during that time, so there's no need to open a new one.

## Following up on a ticket

### Checking status

Go to **Help > My tickets** to see all your tickets, with their status, priority, and the dates they were opened and resolved. The chat assistant can also tell you where things stand.

### Adding information

Reply to the ticket's email or add a comment on the ticket page. Please reply on the same ticket rather than opening a new one, as duplicate tickets can slow things down.

### Reopening a resolved ticket

If the problem comes back within 14 days, reply to the ticket and it'll go back to **Open**. After 14 days, open a new ticket and mention the old ticket number.

## Who can see a ticket

On Starter and Pro, tickets are visible only to the person who opened them and the account owner. On Business, Admins can see them too. Tickets from one account are never visible to another.
